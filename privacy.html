This policy describes the personal UVic OneDrive Backup utility and this informational website. The utility is privately operated and is not affiliated with the University of Victoria.

Access and purpose

The utility requests Google Drive access through OAuth. Its Drive scope permits reading, creating, modifying and deleting Drive files; the backup workflow uses that authorization to inspect existing files and folders, compare content, upload authorized files, and create shortcuts.

Data processed can include file contents, names, folder locations, sizes, timestamps, checksums, Drive item identifiers and shortcut targets. This information is used to maintain the account owner's selected backup and check transfer results.

Storage and retention

Backup content is stored in the owner's Google Drive. Local source files, inventory manifests, checkpoints and transfer logs may retain file metadata needed to operate or verify the backup. OAuth credentials are stored in the owner's local backup configuration.

These records remain under the owner's control until the owner removes them. Revoking access stops future authorized API use but does not automatically remove existing backup files or local records.

Sharing and other uses

File transfers involve the owner's source storage, local computer and Google Drive. The utility does not intentionally publish or share backup content with other people, sell Google user data, use it for advertising, or use it to train general-purpose AI models.

The utility's use of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements.

Owner control

The owner can stop the local utility and revoke its Google access through Google Account connections. The owner manages backup retention and deletion in Google Drive and local storage. The support contact shown on the Google OAuth consent screen is the contact for this personal utility.

This website

These pages contain public documentation only. They do not collect backup files, Google credentials or form submissions. The hosting provider may process ordinary web request data to deliver and secure the site under its own privacy terms.
